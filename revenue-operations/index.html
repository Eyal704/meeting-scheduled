Collections and customer revenue

Control over collections and existing customer revenue

An overdue invoice and a dormant service account need different responses. Custom systems bring together the customer history, assign an owner and show finance and service teams which decisions are outstanding.

Demonstration scenario

A customer says an invoice has been paid

In a collections demonstration, the customer refers to a particular invoice. The account needs to show both what the customer said and what finance still needs to verify.

  1. Identify the invoice

    The account owner can see which invoice the customer means and what happened in previous conversations.

  2. Clarify the response

    A reported payment prompts a request for a reference. A disputed amount is recorded separately with the invoice and the reason.

  3. Finance review

    Finance checks the accounting record and decides whether to close the item, investigate or resume follow-up.

Questions to settle before development

What stays with finance?

Finance defines contact rules, account exceptions, follow-up pauses and payment approval. A customer statement is recorded for verification.

How does this relate to parts and service orders?

On the commercial side, order and asset history help identify customers who have stopped ordering. Service staff review the need and decide whether to quote.

What could we measure?

For collections, track invoices awaiting a decision and time to an assigned owner. For commercial follow-up, track quote requests and confirmed orders.
