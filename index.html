Workflows end to end

From the moment work arrives to the moment it's done

A workflow isn't a feature. It begins when something real happens, gathers the context a good employee would gather, produces work someone can use, and routes the decisions that require judgement to the right person.

Sales engineering

Quoting a technical requirement

  1. Trigger

    A customer sends technical requirements by email.

  2. Context

    Specifications, past quotes, supplier documents, and pricing rules are pulled together.

  3. Draft

    A structured brief and a recommended configuration are written, with assumptions listed.

  4. Your team

    An engineer checks the assumptions, adjusts, and approves.

  5. Outcome

    The proposal goes out and the CRM and quote record are updated.

Field service

Preparing a service visit

  1. Trigger

    A service request comes in, or a machine sends an alert.

  2. Context

    Asset details, previous visits, warranty status, and parts availability are gathered.

  3. Draft

    Likely causes, the checks to run, and the parts to bring are proposed.

  4. Your team

    The technician or planner confirms the plan and the timing.

  5. Outcome

    The visit is scheduled with the right parts, and the service record is updated.

New business

Turning a signal into a conversation

  1. Trigger

    A signal appears — a new role, a tender, a returning visitor, an inbound call.

  2. Context

    The account, the person, and your own history with them are researched.

  3. Draft

    A relevant message is drafted, together with the preparation the rep needs for the call.

  4. Your team

    The rep reviews the message, adjusts it where needed, approves, and sends.

  5. Outcome

    The conversation happens, and the notes and next steps land in the CRM.

The shape repeats across departments. What changes is the trigger, the context that matters, and who signs off — which is exactly the part that has to be designed around how your business actually works.

How we work

Built around your process, not around a demo

The hard part is rarely the model. It's knowing which workflow is worth the effort, how the work really gets done, and where the information actually lives. That understanding is part of what we deliver.

1

Find the right workflow

We look for work that happens often, costs skilled people hours, and has a clear finish line. One workflow, bounded, worth doing.

2

Design it around the real thing

We sit with the people who do the work today — the exceptions, the judgement calls, the steps nobody wrote down — and design around that.

3

Connect the systems

Email, phone, documents, CRM, ERP, and internal knowledge. The workflow reads from where your information already lives and writes back to it.

4

Deploy, measure, improve

We put it in front of real work, watch what it gets wrong, and tighten it. Then we look at the next workflow with everything we learned.
