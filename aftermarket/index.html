Illustrative examples, not reported customer results.

Order gap

A filling-machine customer stops ordering parts

  1. What your records show

    • Seal kits ordered every year, 2019 to 2022
    • No parts order from you since March 2023
    • No open quote, not on the do-not-contact list

  2. The approved message

    “Hi Dana, are you still running your Model X filler? We haven’t supplied a seal kit since March 2023. Would a parts review or quote be useful?”

  3. What your team receives

    • Machine confirmed running, two shifts
    • Asks for a quote on 2 seal kits and a nozzle set
    • Wants delivery before the November shutdown
    • Direct contact details

A gap in your order history doesn’t mean the customer neglected maintenance. The conversation establishes whether there is a current need.

No recent service

An older customer no longer books service

  1. What your records show

    • Three technician visits between 2020 and 2023
    • Nothing booked since
    • The contact on file has left the company

  2. The approved message

    “Hi, I’m reaching out from [Your Company]. We used to service the wrapper at your Ohio plant. Are you now the right person for it, and is it still in operation?”

  3. What your team receives

    • The new maintenance contact
    • Machine status and the issue they describe
    • A preferred window for a visit
    • Everything needed to assess and quote
